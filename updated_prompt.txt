Create a fully editable native draw.io conceptual data model for a generic Payroll Domain.

## Required deliverable

Create this file directly in the current workspace:

`payroll-conceptual-model-crows-foot-editable.drawio`

Do not return only an explanation or a Mermaid diagram. Write the actual `.drawio` file.

The file must:

* Use a valid, uncompressed draw.io `mxfile` and `mxGraphModel` structure.
* Open successfully in app.diagrams.net.
* Contain native editable draw.io shapes and connectors.
* Allow every concept box, definition, relationship and label to be edited independently.
* Use Crow’s Foot data-modelling notation.
* Contain no raster images or embedded screenshots.
* Contain no XYZ wording.
* Use generic Payroll, Workforce and Finance terminology.
* Be suitable for importing into Confluence using the draw.io/diagrams.net plugin.

## Model title

`Payroll Domain — Conceptual Data Model`

Subtitle:

`Business concepts, definitions and relationships using Crow's Foot notation`

## Visual style

Use a clean enterprise data-modelling format.

Each business concept must have:

* A rectangular box.
* A coloured header containing the uppercase concept name.
* A white body containing the business definition.
* A two-pixel coloured border.
* Arial font.
* No gradients, shadows, icons or decorative imagery.
* Square or minimally rounded corners.

## Required business concepts

### WORKER

Colour: Blue.

Definition:

`Represents a person who has a working relationship with an organisation. Workforce owns the worker identity and core details; Payroll references the worker.`

### EMPLOYMENT

Colour: Yellow.

Definition:

`Represents the contractual working relationship between a Worker and an employing organisation, including effective dates and status. One Worker may have multiple Employments.`

### PAYROLL ENROLMENT

Colour: Purple.

Definition:

`Connects an eligible Employment to a payroll arrangement. It determines when and how that Employment is included in payroll processing.`

### PAYROLL INPUT

Colour: Green.

Definition:

`Represents an amount, time value, instruction or event used to calculate pay. Examples include salary, overtime, allowance, absence, tax, pension and retrospective adjustment.`

### PAYROLL RUN

Colour: Blue.

Definition:

`Represents an execution of payroll calculation for a defined Payroll Period. It applies payroll rules and inputs to eligible Payroll Enrolments and records processing status, exceptions and approval.`

### PAYROLL PERIOD

Colour: Yellow.

Definition:

`Defines the time interval for which pay is calculated and reported, including period start, period end, cut-off and payment date.`

### PAYROLL RESULT

Colour: Purple.

Definition:

`Represents the calculated payroll outcome for a Payroll Enrolment and Payroll Run, including earnings, deductions, employer contributions, gross pay, balances and net pay.`

### PAYMENT

Colour: Green.

Definition:

`Represents settlement of approved net pay, including payment instruction, amount, method, date and status. Failed or reversed Payments remain traceable.`

### COST ALLOCATION

Colour: Blue.

Definition:

`Represents distribution of payroll expenditure to Finance structures such as Cost Centre, Organisation Unit or Programme for accounting and reconciliation.`

### PAYROLL REPORTING

Colour: Yellow.

Definition:

`Represents outputs derived from approved Payroll Results, including payslips, reconciliation and statutory submissions to authorised external organisations.`

## Layout

Use a landscape layout with four levels.

### Level 1

Place horizontally:

`WORKER → EMPLOYMENT → PAYROLL ENROLMENT`

### Level 2

Place:

* Payroll Input on the left.
* Payroll Run in the centre.
* Payroll Period on the right.

Payroll Enrolment must connect down to Payroll Run.

### Level 3

Place Payroll Result centrally below Payroll Run.

Payroll Result must be connected to both Payroll Run and Payroll Enrolment.

### Level 4

Place horizontally:

* Payment on the left.
* Cost Allocation in the centre.
* Payroll Reporting on the right.

All three must connect to Payroll Result.

Use straight or orthogonal connectors. Avoid line crossings and text overlap.

## Crow’s Foot relationships

Use draw.io Entity Relationship arrow styles such as:

* `ERone`
* `ERzeroToOne`
* `ERoneToMany`
* `ERzeroToMany`

Use Crow’s Foot markers at both ends where required. Do not use ordinary triangular arrows as a substitute.

Create these relationships:

### Worker to Employment

Business sentence:

`A Worker has one or more Employments. Each Employment belongs to exactly one Worker.`

Source marker at Worker: exactly one.

Target marker at Employment: one or many.

Relationship label: `has`

### Employment to Payroll Enrolment

Business sentence:

`An Employment may have zero or many Payroll Enrolments. Each Payroll Enrolment belongs to exactly one Employment.`

Source marker at Employment: exactly one.

Target marker at Payroll Enrolment: zero or many.

Relationship label: `has`

### Payroll Enrolment to Payroll Run

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Enrolment may be processed by many Payroll Runs, and a Payroll Run processes many Payroll Enrolments.`

Use zero-or-many notation at both ends.

Relationship label: `is processed by`

### Payroll Input to Payroll Run

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Run may use many Payroll Inputs, and an input may be used during original or repeat processing.`

Use zero-or-many notation at both ends.

Relationship label: `is used by`

### Payroll Period to Payroll Run

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Period may schedule zero or many Payroll Runs. Each Payroll Run belongs to exactly one Payroll Period.`

Source marker at Payroll Period: exactly one.

Target marker at Payroll Run: zero or many.

Relationship label: `schedules`

### Payroll Run to Payroll Result

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Run produces one or many Payroll Results. Each Payroll Result is produced by exactly one Payroll Run.`

Source marker at Payroll Run: exactly one.

Target marker at Payroll Result: one or many.

Relationship label: `produces`

### Payroll Enrolment to Payroll Result

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Enrolment may receive zero or many Payroll Results. Each Payroll Result belongs to exactly one Payroll Enrolment.`

Source marker at Payroll Enrolment: exactly one.

Target marker at Payroll Result: zero or many.

Relationship label: `receives`

### Payroll Result to Payment

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Result may authorise zero or many Payments. Each Payment must reference exactly one Payroll Result.`

Source marker at Payroll Result: exactly one.

Target marker at Payment: zero or many.

Relationship label: `authorises`

### Payroll Result to Cost Allocation

Business sentence:

`A Payroll Result may produce zero or many Cost Allocations. Each Cost Allocation references exactly one Payroll Result.`

Source marker at Payroll Result: exactly one.

Target marker at Cost Allocation: zero or many.

Relationship label: `produces`

### Payroll Result to Payroll Reporting

Business sentence:

`Payroll Results support Payroll Reporting outputs. A report or statutory submission may contain information from many Payroll Results.`

Use suitable many-to-many notation.

Relationship label: `supports`
